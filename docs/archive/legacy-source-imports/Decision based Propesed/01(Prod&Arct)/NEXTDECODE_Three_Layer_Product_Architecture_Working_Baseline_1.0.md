# NEXTDECODE — Three-Layer Product Architecture
## Working Baseline 1.0

**Purpose:** Capture the working product structure for Creator Success Intelligence, the Shared Intelligence Core, and Specialized Studios & Channels.

**Status:** Working architecture baseline  
**Scope:** Product and conceptual architecture only  
**Implementation:** Not authorized  
**Date:** 31 October 2026

---

## 1. Product architecture at a glance

NEXTDECODE is organized into three connected layers:

1. **Creator Success Intelligence (CSI):** The intelligence that understands audiences, discovers opportunities, learns, and improves content and business outcomes.
2. **Shared Intelligence Core:** Reusable intelligence, knowledge, evidence, governance, and orchestration capabilities shared across studios.
3. **Specialized Studios and Channels:** Format-specific environments that turn ideas and evidence into content and distribute it through approved channels.

Central principle:

> One shared intelligence system. Multiple content studios. Multiple distribution channels. One unified operating environment.

These are connected layers, not three independent products and not eight disconnected AI agents.

---

## 2. Layer 1 — Creator Success Intelligence

CSI is the strategic intelligence layer, organized into eight domains:

### 2.1 Audience Intelligence
Understands audience needs, questions, interests, behavior, segments, and changes over time.

### 2.2 Opportunity Intelligence
Discovers and evaluates content and business opportunities using audience value, differentiation, brand fit, evidence, feasibility, and business potential.

### 2.3 Creative Intelligence
Develops creative directions, concepts, hooks, scripts, storytelling approaches, and format-specific creative recommendations within approved editorial policies.

### 2.4 Production Intelligence
Coordinates production planning, resource requirements, execution feasibility, quality signals, and production learning across studios.

### 2.5 Packaging Intelligence
Improves titles, thumbnails, descriptions, hooks, metadata, and platform-specific presentation while preserving factual meaning and brand integrity.

### 2.6 Distribution Intelligence
Recommends suitable channels, formats, adaptations, schedules, and distribution strategies. A recommendation or schedule is not publishing authorization.

### 2.7 Performance Intelligence
Measures content and audience outcomes, supports controlled experimentation, distinguishes correlation from causation, and promotes validated learning.

### 2.8 Business Intelligence
Connects monetization opportunities, revenue, expenses, profitability, attribution, and business performance while protecting audience trust and editorial integrity.

**Design principle:** CSI should be implemented as a shared intelligence capability with specialized services—not as eight isolated agents with duplicated data and policies.

---

## 3. Layer 2 — Shared Intelligence Core

The Shared Intelligence Core provides common capabilities used by all intelligence domains and studios:

- **Content Graph and Knowledge Management:** Connects content, variants, evidence, claims, assets, audiences, decisions, and learning.
- **Research and Evidence:** Manages source provenance, claims, verification, freshness, confidence, and contradictions.
- **Brand Intelligence:** Maintains parent-brand positioning, principles, voice, visual identity, and studio-specific editorial identities.
- **Strategy and Planning:** Connects opportunities, priorities, content portfolios, capacity, and business objectives.
- **Content Quality and Rights:** Applies factual, editorial, creative, production, brand, and rights requirements.
- **Agent Intelligence and Orchestration:** Registers agent capabilities, routes work, manages scoped permissions, and coordinates recoverable execution.
- **Resource and Governance Management:** Tracks provider capabilities, quotas, budgets, authorization, policy versions, approvals, and emergency controls.
- **Analytics and Learning Registry:** Preserves performance observations, experiments, validated learnings, confidence, applicability, and version history.

The Core is shared infrastructure for intelligence and governance. Studios may specialize their workflows, but they should not create conflicting copies of shared evidence, permissions, or policy.

---

## 4. Layer 3 — Specialized Studios and Channels

Studios provide format-specific creation and production workflows. Distribution channels provide destinations and audience touchpoints.

### Initial and planned studios

- **Shorts Studio:** Existing specialized Python/FFmpeg production engine. Integrate it; do not replace it wholesale.
- **Long-form Studio:** Research-led long-form planning, scripting, storytelling, production, and review.
- **Social and X Studio:** Platform-specific short-form text, visual, and campaign workflows.
- **Blog and Newsletter Studio:** Written editorial content and newsletter workflows.
- **Future studios:** Podcast and other formats when justified by strategy and capacity.

### Distribution and business touchpoints

Studios may prepare content for relevant channels and destinations. Channel-specific rules, permissions, and publishing governance remain centralized.

Distribution preparation, recommendation, and scheduling do not independently authorize public publishing.

---

## 5. Unified operating and learning loop

The ecosystem operates through a continuous loop:

1. Discover audience needs.
2. Evaluate opportunities.
3. Research and verify evidence.
4. Plan creative direction.
5. Create format-specific content.
6. Validate quality and rights.
7. Distribute within authorization.
8. Measure audience and business outcomes.
9. Learn and improve.

Validated learning can improve future opportunity selection, creative decisions, production methods, packaging, distribution, and business planning.

---

## 6. Cross-layer operating rules

### 6.1 Shared intelligence, specialized execution
Audience context, evidence, brand knowledge, governance, and validated learning are shared. Editorial execution, creative treatment, production configuration, and format-specific QA remain studio-aware.

### 6.2 Evidence reuse does not mean content duplication
Research and evidence may be reused across formats with provenance preserved. Each format must create its own appropriate storytelling and independently pass its applicable quality and rights checks.

### 6.3 Recommendations are not permissions
An AI recommendation, workflow schedule, completed production run, or editorial approval does not itself grant authorization for a sensitive external action.

### 6.4 Governance applies across all layers
Rights, security, privacy, financial authority, policy controls, approval boundaries, auditability, and emergency pause must apply consistently across the ecosystem.

### 6.5 Learning must be evidence-based
A single successful publication must not automatically become a permanent strategic rule. Learning records need evidence, context, confidence, applicability, and version history.

## 8. Important implementation boundaries

This document records product structure. It is not proof that all described capabilities already exist.

The current Shorts Studio remains a specialized production engine and should be integrated through explicit contracts. Existing architecture and Phase 10/11 reports remain the evidence sources for current-state claims; no fresh repository inspection is represented here.

No permission is granted by this document for:
- Repository inspection or Git operations.
- Source-code, test, or configuration changes.
- Dependency installation or environment changes.
- Cloud provisioning, paid services, or deployment.
- Production generation, uploads, or public publishing.
- Changes to existing agent ownership.

Implementation requires separate explicit authorization.
